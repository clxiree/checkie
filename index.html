Questions students ask

Does Checkie tell me if a listing is safe?

No. It shows which common scam patterns appear and what to check. A listing with no warning signs can still be a scam, so always see the room (or a live video call) and read a contract before paying.

Why not just ask a senior, or a chatbot?

Do ask seniors too: they know the city. Checkie adds a consistent check of 12 common scam patterns and typical prices for 12 areas, any time of day. Unlike pasting a listing into a chatbot, your answers stay on your device, and Checkie gives you ready-made messages for the landlord (English or Spanish) and your university.

What does Checkie save about me?

Nothing from the check itself: your answers stay in your browser, and your chosen campus is remembered only on your device. If you choose to send feedback, what you type is saved, together with your result level, how many screenshots you added and, unless you untick the box, which warning signs you answered Yes or Not sure to. It never includes your name, email or the listing.         Renting guide · Barcelona

        Finding and confirming a room: 8 steps

        Go through these before you pay anything.

        1💶 Set your budget and area

        Most students pay for a single room in a shared flat. Use the price map to see what rooms usually cost in each area, and add about €80–120 a month if bills are not included.

        • Pick 2–3 areas that fit your budget and your commute
        • Ask whether bills (electricity, water, internet) are included
        2🔎 Search where you are protected

        Platforms such as HousingAnywhere, Spotahome and Uniplaces hold your first payment until after you move in, but only if you book and pay on their site. Listings in Facebook groups or WhatsApp have no protection.

        • Prefer listings you can book and pay through the platform
        • If you find a room elsewhere, be extra careful with every step below
        3🖼️ Check the listing before you reply

        Scam listings often copy real photos and prices.

        • Compare the price with the area (a price far below it is a warning sign)
        • Search the photos with a reverse image search (Google Lens or TinEye)
        • Look up the street on Google Street View
        4💬 Talk to the landlord, and keep it on the platform

        Stay in the platform's messages, where there are records and moderation. Be careful if they push you to WhatsApp or email.

        • Ask for a viewing in person, or a live video call where they show the room
        • Ask for the exact address, floor and door number
        • Be wary of a landlord who is 'abroad' and will send the keys
        5🪪 Check who you are renting from

        Make sure the person you pay is the owner, or an agency acting for them.

        • Ask for the landlord's full name; it should match the contract and the bank account
        • You can order a nota simple from Spain's Property Registry (registradores.org) to see who owns the flat
        • Don't send your passport or ID until you have seen the room and are signing
        6📄 Read a written contract before paying

        The contract should name the landlord, the exact address, the rent, the deposit, what is included and the dates.

        • Never pay before you have read the contract
        • Ask what any fee is for: for a standard home rental, Spain's 2023 housing law says the landlord pays the agency's fees
        • A deposit (fianza) of one to two months' rent is typical; be wary of more upfront
        7🏦 Pay safely, and keep proof

        Pay by bank transfer to an account in the landlord's or agency's name, ideally a Spanish account.

        • Never use Western Union, crypto or gift cards
        • Keep the contract, receipts and all messages
        • Don't pay because of pressure ('5 people want it today')
        8🔑 Move in, and act fast if something is wrong

        On the platforms above you usually have only 24–48 hours after move-in to report a problem.

        • Take photos of the room's condition on day one
        • Ask whether you can register (empadronarte) at the address
        • If you paid and it was a scam: report it to the platform, your bank and the Mossos d'Esquadra (Catalan police), and tell your university's international office
        ✋ Something feels wrong? Stop before paying. Checkie's result page can help you write to your university's international office.

        Already paid and think it's a scam?

        1. Contact your bank now. Ask them to stop or recall the transfer. The sooner you call, the better the chance.
        2. Stop all payments and contact. Don't send more money, ID or documents, even if they promise a refund.
        3. Keep the evidence. Screenshots of the listing, messages, payment receipts and the landlord's details.
        4. Report it to the platform where you found the listing, and to the police: in Catalonia, the Mossos d'Esquadra (in person or online).
        5. Tell your university's international office. They can help with emergency housing. Get their contact.

        Words you'll see in Spanish listings

        Fianza
        Deposit, usually one to two months' rent, returned when you leave if there's no damage.
        Contrato de alquiler / de temporada
        Rental contract. A "temporada" contract is for a fixed period, such as a semester.
        Gastos incluidos
        Bills included (electricity, water, internet). Ask exactly which ones.
        Nota simple
        A Property Registry document showing who owns a flat. You can order it online.
        Empadronamiento
        Registering your address with the city hall. You need it for some procedures, so ask if the landlord allows it.
        NIE
        Spain's foreigner identity number. Some landlords ask for it; only share ID once you've seen the room and are signing.

        General guidance, not legal advice. Platform rules from their own help pages (HousingAnywhere, Spotahome, Uniplaces), checked October 2026.

        The problem

        Incoming students often search for rooms from abroad, on platforms, in Facebook groups and on WhatsApp, before they know local prices or rental practices. Common scams exploit that gap: a deposit before any viewing, a landlord "abroad", pressure to pay today. When it goes wrong, students may arrive without a room and turn to your office.

        Evidence so far, and its limits

        In my own early conversations (October 2026), 12 of 15 exchange and international students at Esade and UPF said they had met scam warning signs while looking for a room, and most checked listings by asking seniors. This is a small sample from my own network, not a representative survey. A pilot would test it properly.

        Beyond a housing resources page

        • It works on the student's specific listing, not general advice: 12 questions about what this landlord said and asked for, with what to do for each warning sign.
        • It checks the price against typical ranges for 12 Barcelona areas, and for residences and studios.
        • It gives the student words to use: a message to the landlord in English or Spanish, and a ready-made message to your office that lists what was flagged.
        • It's available when students search, often outside office hours, on their phone.

        Strongest use case: one link in your pre-arrival email, so students check a listing before paying a deposit and arrive at your office, if needed, with a clear question.
